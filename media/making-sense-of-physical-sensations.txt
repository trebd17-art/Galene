Have you ever noticed your heart beating faster, your shoulders tightening, or your stomach feeling unsettled when you’re anxious?

Anxiety can affect both your thoughts and your body. When your body prepares for a possible threat, you might notice sweating, tense muscles, or a faster heartbeat.

Those sensations can feel uncomfortable. But a sensation alone cannot tell you what is causing it. Physical symptoms can have other causes, too.

Imagine you’re having a busy afternoon and notice tight shoulders. You might pause, adjust your posture, or take a short break.

If it feels comfortable, notice a neutral point of contact—like your hand resting on a chair or your feet touching the floor.

If focusing on your body feels unpleasant, look around instead. Notice the color of a nearby object. You can also skip this exercise.

You don’t have to make every sensation disappear. The aim is to give yourself a moment to notice what’s happening and choose your next step.

Avoid assuming that a new or concerning symptom is only anxiety. Seek medical advice when you’re unsure, and urgent medical help for severe or potentially urgent symptoms.
