The room gets quiet, and suddenly your mind gets busy.
You remember tomorrow’s appointment. An unfinished task. A conversation you wish had gone differently.
With fewer distractions, worries can become more noticeable.
If this happens to you, consider setting aside a brief moment earlier in the evening to write down what needs attention.
Keep it simple: ‘Check the appointment time tomorrow.’
For a practical concern, you might add one next step and when you’ll take it.
You don’t need a detailed review of everything that could go wrong.
Later, if the same reminder appears in bed, you can gently acknowledge it: ‘I’ve made a note. There’s a time to return to this.’
That doesn’t mean the thought will disappear. You’re giving yourself an option besides repeatedly working through it.
If writing makes you more alert, skip it or try it earlier.
And you don’t have to demand a completely empty mind before resting.
If worries or sleep difficulties keep affecting your daily life, consider speaking with a healthcare professional.
For tonight, see whether one concern can wait for a more useful time.
