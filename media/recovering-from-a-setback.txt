You were building a routine. Then you missed a day. Maybe two.
Suddenly, your mind says, ‘I’ve ruined it. There’s no point continuing.’
A setback can feel discouraging, especially when the goal matters to you. But one attempt gives you information about that attempt. It doesn’t describe everything you’re capable of.
Imagine planning a daily walk and missing several days during a demanding week.
Before deciding you failed, ask: What made this difficult?
Was the timing unrealistic? Were you exhausted? Did something unexpected need your attention?
Then ask: What could make another attempt more manageable?
You might choose a shorter walk, a different time, or someone to join you.
Sometimes the useful adjustment is rest. Sometimes the goal itself needs to change.
You don’t need to make up for a missed day by demanding twice as much tomorrow.
Think about one plan that hasn’t gone as expected. What would a fair restart look like?
Your earlier efforts still happened. You can learn from this moment and decide what comes next.
