You send a message. Time passes. There’s no reply.
Before long, your mind starts filling in the silence.
‘They’re upset with me.’
‘I must have said something wrong.’
Those thoughts can feel convincing—especially when you’re anxious. But feeling certain and having evidence are different things.
Here’s a useful distinction.
‘They haven’t replied yet’ is something you can observe.
‘They’re upset with me’ is one possible explanation. It hasn’t been confirmed.
Try asking yourself two questions: What do I actually know? And what am I adding to the story?
You don’t have to replace a worrying prediction with a cheerful one. ‘Everything is definitely fine’ is also more than you know.
A balanced response might be: ‘I haven’t heard back, and I don’t know why yet.’
Then consider whether anything needs your attention now—or whether you can return to another part of your day.
Your feelings deserve care. Your predictions deserve a little room for questioning.
You can take a thought seriously without treating it as a fact.
