Think about the last time you made a mistake.
What did you say to yourself?
Maybe it was, ‘I ruin everything,’ or ‘I should be better than this.’
A harsh inner voice can turn one difficult moment into a judgment about your whole identity.
Supportive self-talk can be more accurate—and more useful.
Imagine you missed an important detail at work or school.
‘I’m hopeless’ gives you very little direction.
‘I missed that detail. I can correct it and make a reminder for next time’ acknowledges what happened and leaves room for action.
Try imagining what you would say to someone you care about in the same situation.
Would you tell them one mistake defines them? Or would you help them understand what happened and what they could do next?
See whether one fair sentence could apply to you, too.
You don’t have to believe a glowing statement about yourself. Start with something believable.
‘This is difficult.’
‘I’m still learning.’
‘I can take responsibility and ask for help.’
A supportive inner voice makes room for honesty, repair, and another attempt.
