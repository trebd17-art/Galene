It would feel reassuring to know exactly how things will turn out.
Will the interview go well? Will they like my idea? Am I making the right choice?
Preparation can help. But some answers only become available after we take a step.
Making room for uncertainty means recognizing the limits of what you can know while still taking sensible precautions.
Imagine preparing for an interview. You review the role, practice a few answers, and check the meeting time.
You still can’t know every question in advance.
At that point, more checking may stop adding useful information.
You might try: ‘I don’t know exactly how this will go. I can prepare reasonably and respond as I go.’
Choose a small, everyday situation where the risks are manageable. Ask what information you need for a reasonable decision—and what would be an impossible guarantee.
You don’t have to feel completely comfortable before deciding.
If repeated checking or asking for reassurance feels distressing or difficult to control, professional support can help.
For today, leave a little space for ‘I don’t know yet.’ A reasonable next step can still be available.
