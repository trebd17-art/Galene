Have you ever spent a long time thinking about a problem without getting any closer to a decision?
Sometimes thinking helps us prepare. Sometimes it keeps bringing us back to the same unanswered question.
Imagine you’re getting ready for an appointment.
Checking the address and choosing when to leave are useful planning.
Replaying every possible delay after you’ve made your plan may become a worry loop.
The difference isn’t simply how long you think. Some problems need time. Ask whether your thinking is adding information or helping you choose an action.
Try this question: ‘Is there one practical step available to me?’
If there is, make it specific. Set a reminder. Ask a question. Write down when you’ll handle it.
If there isn’t, you can acknowledge the concern without continuing to solve it right now.
You might say, ‘I’ve done the planning available to me. I can come back if something changes.’
Then gently return to the task or person in front of you.
The worry may return. That doesn’t mean you failed. You can notice it and choose where to place your attention again.
