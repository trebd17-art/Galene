When something makes you anxious, avoiding it can feel like a relief.

Maybe you skip a social invitation because you’re worried about an awkward conversation. In the moment, staying home feels easier.

Sometimes, though, repeatedly avoiding a feared situation can make it harder to return. You may have fewer chances to learn how you could handle it.

That doesn’t mean you should force yourself into the hardest situation—or enter a situation that is unsafe.

A small, manageable step may be a better place to begin.

For example, after an uncomfortable conversation, you might consider a brief call with someone you trust rather than a large gathering.

Take a moment to think about one safe, low-pressure step you could consider. Or think of someone you could ask for support.

You don’t need to act on it right now.

Getting support is also a meaningful step. If anxiety keeps affecting your sleep, relationships, work, school, or everyday activities, consider talking with a qualified mental health professional.

They can help you understand what’s happening and choose an approach suited to you.

Progress doesn’t have to mean feeling no anxiety. It can mean making space for something that matters to you, with support when you need it.

You don’t have to handle everything alone.
